# P526

Source file: `forms-instructions-and-publications/publications/p526.pdf`

Title: `Publication 526 - Charitable Contributions`

Role in the tax program:

- primary individual guide for charitable contribution deduction
- drives:
  - whether recipient qualifies
  - whether contribution type qualifies
  - deduction limits
  - substantiation requirements
  - noncash reporting workflow

Core rule:

- charitable contribution deduction is generally claimed only if taxpayer itemizes on `Schedule A`

Basic contribution definition:

- donation or gift to or for the use of a qualified organization
- voluntary
- made without receiving equal value in return

Key 2025 items called out:

- contributions to certain federally chartered veteran service organizations under `501(c)(19)` are deductible even if membership is not primarily wartime veterans
- one-time qualified charitable distribution election:
  - up to `54,000`
  - from IRA
  - through certain charitable remainder / gift annuity structures
- noncash deduction over `5,000` generally requires `Form 8283 Section B`

Qualified organization rules:

- taxpayer can deduct only if recipient is a qualified organization
- qualification can be checked through `IRS.gov/TEOS`
- common categories include:
  - religious
  - charitable
  - educational
  - scientific
  - literary
  - cruelty-prevention organizations
  - certain governmental units for public purposes
  - certain veterans’ organizations

Important disallowance / limit implications:

- not every payment to a nonprofit is deductible
- quid-pro-quo style payments are not fully deductible
- some conservation contributions are disallowed in partnership / S corp settings
- noncash contributions have special valuation and substantiation rules

Main supporting forms/publications:

- `Schedule A`
- `Form 8283`
- `Pub. 561`
- `Pub. 590-B` for QCD interactions

High-value modeling distinctions:

- cash vs noncash contributions
- qualified recipient vs nonqualified recipient
- public-purpose governmental gift vs private-purpose payment
- charitable gift vs tuition / fee substitute / benefit purchase
- ordinary charitable deduction vs QCD pathway

Substantiation implications:

- records matter materially
- noncash gifts over thresholds require additional reporting
- donated-property valuation is its own rule set; do not guess values inside the core engine

Model-building takeaways:

- keep charitable contributions categorized by:
  - cash
  - noncash
  - recipient type
  - substantiation level
  - valuation source
- route donated-property valuation problems to `Pub. 561`
- preserve QCD facts separately from ordinary Schedule A charity
