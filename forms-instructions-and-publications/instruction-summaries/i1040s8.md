# I1040S8

Source file: `forms-instructions-and-publications/instructions/i1040s8.pdf`

Title:

- `Instructions for Schedule 8812 (Form 1040)`
- credits for qualifying children and other dependents

High-value implementation takeaways:

- this is the main source for `Schedule 8812` child tax credit (`CTC`), additional child tax credit (`ACTC`), and credit for other dependents (`ODC`) routing
- the source sharply distinguishes taxpayer-ID requirements for `CTC` / `ACTC` versus `ODC`, so the model must preserve those identification facts separately
- refundability for `ACTC` is not just a generic refundable-credit toggle; it depends on the schedule's specific earned-income and qualifying-child tests

2025 items surfaced early in the instructions:

- a filer claiming `CTC` or `ACTC` must have a valid `SSN`; on a joint return, only one spouse must have a valid `SSN`, while the other spouse may have an `SSN` or `ITIN` issued by the due date
- the maximum `CTC` per qualifying child increased to `2,200`
- the maximum `ACTC` per qualifying child is `1,700`
- the instructions reference new `Trump account` / `Form 4547` elections for certain children born after `2024` and before `2029`
- the IRS warns that refunds on returns properly claiming `ACTC` cannot be issued before mid-February `2026`

Eligibility and identification implications:

- for `CTC` / `ACTC`, a qualifying child must have a valid employment-authorized `SSN` issued by the due date of the return, including extensions
- if a child lacks a valid `SSN` but has another qualifying taxpayer identification number issued by the due date, the child may still support `ODC` even though they cannot support `CTC` / `ACTC`
- if a dependent lacks an `SSN`, `ITIN`, or `ATIN` issued by the due date, that dependent cannot be used for `ODC`
- if a child was born and died in `2025` without an `SSN`, the instructions call for supporting records such as a birth certificate, death certificate, or hospital records

Model-building implications:

- taxpayer and dependent identifier facts should not be flattened into a single generic `has_tin` flag
- the schedule needs enough dependent detail to distinguish qualifying-child treatment from other-dependent treatment
- refund timing notes belong to workflow / filing guidance, but the underlying `ACTC` amount logic belongs in the computation model
- any `Schedule 8812` implementation should preserve the path from dependent facts to `CTC`, `ODC`, and refundable `ACTC` instead of collapsing them into one credit amount
