# P970

Source file: `forms-instructions-and-publications/publications/p970.pdf`

Title: `Publication 970 - Tax Benefits for Education`

Role in the tax program:

- umbrella publication for education-related tax benefits
- central for education credits, student-loan-interest deduction, scholarships, 529 / savings-bond interactions, employer educational assistance, and education-related exclusions

Major benefit areas:

- scholarships and fellowships
- American opportunity credit
- lifetime learning credit
- student loan interest deduction
- tuition program / bond / savings interactions
- employer educational assistance
- work-related education

2025 front-matter values:

- student loan interest deduction phases out:
  - `85,000` to `100,000`
  - `170,000` to `200,000` MFJ
- education savings bond exclusion phases out:
  - `99,500` to `114,500`
  - `149,250` to `179,250` MFJ
- work-related education mileage rate: `70 cents`

Current major reminders:

- `Form 1098-T` is generally required for AOTC / LLC eligibility, with limited exceptions
- educational institution EIN required for AOTC on `Form 8863`
- `Form 8862` may be required after prior disallowance
- invalid claims can trigger 2-year or 10-year ban
- taxpayer and student generally need TIN by due date for AOTC
- emergency higher-education grants generally not included in income and do not necessarily reduce creditable expenses
- choosing to include otherwise tax-free scholarships in income can sometimes improve education credits
- student loan forgiveness rules for 2021-2025 discharges noted

Special 2025 item:

- `Trump account` and `Form 4547` are noted here too because of education/family-benefit overlap

Critical modeling distinctions:

- same dollar of tuition cannot support multiple inconsistent benefits
- scholarships can be tax-free or strategically included in income depending on optimization
- credits vs deductions vs exclusions must be coordinated

Model-building takeaways:

- preserve:
  - student identity and dependency status
  - school and `1098-T` data
  - qualified expenses actually paid
  - scholarships/grants
  - enrollment status / year-in-school facts
  - student loan interest
  - 529 / bond / employer-assistance interactions
- education optimization should be a coordination engine, not isolated form-by-form math
