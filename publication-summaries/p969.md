# P969

Source file: `forms-instructions-and-publications/publications/p969.pdf`

Title: `Publication 969 - Health Savings Accounts and Other Tax-Favored Health Plans`

Role in the tax program:

- main guide for HSAs, Archer MSAs, Medicare Advantage MSAs, health FSAs, and HRAs
- relevant for above-the-line HSA deductions, employer contributions, medical-expense reimbursements, and disqualifying-coverage checks

Programs covered:

- HSA
- Archer MSA
- Medicare Advantage MSA
- health FSA
- HRA

Core HSA mechanics:

- eligible individual may deduct contributions whether or not itemizing
- employer contributions are excluded from income
- distributions for qualified medical expenses are tax-free

Core plan distinctions:

- HSA can receive contributions from eligible individual or others
- Archer MSA cannot receive both employer and individual contributions in same year
- Medicare Advantage MSA contributions come only from Medicare
- HRA is employer-funded only

Current 2025 front-matter items:

- telehealth/remote care can be disregarded coverage for HSA eligibility in applicable plan years after 2024
- health FSA salary reduction limit: `3,300`
- max FSA carryover: `660`
- preventive-care guidance expanded
- condoms treated as medical care expenses and eligible for reimbursement under various plans

Important modeling distinctions:

- HSA eligibility depends on HDHP status and other coverage
- reimbursed expense cannot also be separately deducted under section 213
- contributions, employer exclusions, distributions, and penalties are separate flows

Model-building takeaways:

- capture:
  - HDHP coverage months
  - self-only vs family coverage
  - employer contributions
  - taxpayer/spouse contributions
  - distributions and whether used for qualified expenses
  - FSA/HRA or other coverage that may affect HSA eligibility
- keep HSA deduction logic coordinated with medical-expense deduction logic to prevent double benefit
