# P547

Source file: `forms-instructions-and-publications/publications/p547.pdf`

Title: `Publication 547 - Casualties, Disasters, and Thefts`

Role in the tax program:

- governs casualty/theft loss and gain treatment
- important for disaster-year returns, reimbursements, personal-vs-business property distinctions, and `Form 4684`

Core covered topics:

- casualty
- theft
- loss on deposits
- proof of loss
- figuring loss
- deduction limits
- figuring gain
- timing and reporting
- disaster-area special rules

Current high-value rules:

- personal casualty/theft losses for individuals are generally deductible only if attributable to a federally declared disaster
- theft losses in transactions entered into for profit may still be deductible
- ordinary personal casualty losses generally subject to:
  - `100` per-casualty reduction
  - `10%` of AGI reduction
- qualified disaster losses instead:
  - not subject to `10%` AGI reduction
  - use `500` reduction instead

2025 items called out:

- disaster-related postponements now count as filing extensions for refund/credit lookback purposes in certain cases
- mandatory postponement expanded for certain federally declared and qualified state-declared disasters after `2025-07-24`
- automatic disaster extension increased from `60` to `120` days in affected cases
- qualified disaster loss relief extended for certain major disasters
- losses from financial scams entered into for profit may qualify as theft loss deduction

Other relief items mentioned:

- certain wildfire relief payments are nontaxable
- certain East Palestine derailment relief payments are nontaxable

Core modeling distinctions:

- casualty vs theft vs deposit loss
- personal-use vs business / income-producing property
- reimbursed vs unreimbursed loss
- federally declared disaster vs nonqualified event
- deductible loss vs taxable gain after reimbursement

Main forms / workbooks:

- `Form 4684`
- `Schedule A`
- `Schedule D`
- `Form 4797`
- `Pub. 584`
- `Pub. 584-B`

Important interactions:

- condemnations / involuntary conversions link to `Pub. 544`
- basis reduction / measurement links to `Pub. 551`
- amended return may be required in retroactive disaster-relief situations

Model-building takeaways:

- preserve event-level facts:
  - event type
  - disaster declaration status
  - date
  - property type
  - basis / FMV before-after
  - insurance and other reimbursements
  - prior-year election / amended-return facts
- disaster relief logic should not be hard-coded as one general loss rule; the declaration status changes deduction mechanics materially
