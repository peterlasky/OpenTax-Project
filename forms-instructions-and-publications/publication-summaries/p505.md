# P505

Source file: `forms-instructions-and-publications/publications/p505.pdf`

Title: `Publication 505 - Tax Withholding and Estimated Tax`

Role in the tax program:

- primary guide for pay-as-you-go tax mechanics
- drives withholding assumptions, estimated-tax obligations, safe-harbor style reasoning, and underpayment penalty workflow
- relevant to both ordinary income tax and add-on taxes like SE tax, Additional Medicare Tax, and NIIT

Core rule:

- federal tax is pay-as-you-go
- payment happens through:
  - withholding
  - estimated tax

Main build implications:

- do not treat tax due only at filing time
- model withholding and estimates as separate payment streams from liability computation
- underpayment penalty is a distinct downstream calculation

2025 items called out near the front:

- social security wage base: `176,100`
- Additional Medicare Tax thresholds:
  - single / HOH / QSS: `200,000`
  - MFJ: `250,000`
  - MFS: `125,000`
- NIIT thresholds:
  - single / HOH / QSS: `200,000`
  - MFJ: `250,000`
  - MFS: `125,000`
- advance PTC changes during the year should be reported to Marketplace

Withholding path:

- applies to wages and can also apply to certain other income
- withholding can be adjusted through `Form W-4`
- publication is especially relevant when taxpayer wants to:
  - increase withholding instead of making estimates
  - reconcile excess social security / RRTA style withholding
  - handle pension / bonus / gambling / other withholding situations

Estimated-tax path:

- usually relevant when withholding is absent or insufficient
- especially relevant for:
  - self-employment income
  - dividends
  - interest
  - capital gains
  - rents
  - royalties
- estimated tax may need to cover:
  - income tax
  - self-employment tax
  - AMT
  - Additional Medicare Tax
  - NIIT

Penalty implications:

- if taxpayer underpays during the year, penalty may apply
- IRS can often compute penalty, but the tax engine should still preserve:
  - quarterly due dates
  - withholding timing assumptions
  - estimated payment dates / amounts
  - safe-harbor comparison inputs

Forms and workflow relevance:

- `Form W-4`
- `Form 1040-ES`
- `Form 2210`
- `Form 8962`
- `Form W-7` for ITIN renewal issues tied to estimated payments
- `Form 1040-SS` has its own estimated-tax worksheet path

Model-building takeaways:

- keep payment logic separate from tax-liability logic
- preserve dated estimated-tax-payment detail, not just one annual total
- allow withholding as both:
  - source-form-driven amounts
  - deliberate planning lever via increased wage withholding
- include Additional Medicare Tax and NIIT in estimate planning, not only in final return math
