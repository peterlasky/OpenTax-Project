# I1040SCA

Source file: `forms-instructions-and-publications/instructions/i1040sca.pdf`

Title: `Instructions for Schedule A (Form 1040) - Itemized Deductions`

Role in the tax program:

- governs the itemized-deduction branch
- controls SALT limitation, mortgage-interest routing, charitable contributions, and several publication cross-references

Important 2025 change called out at the top:

- SALT deduction limit increased to `40,000`
- SALT deduction limit is `20,000` for MFS
- the overall limit is reduced if modified AGI is more than `500,000`
- the overall limit is reduced if modified AGI is more than `250,000` for MFS
- the reduced limit may not fall below `10,000`
- the reduced limit may not fall below `5,000` for MFS

Implementation implications:

- `f1040sa.5e` cannot stop at a simple fixed cap if high-income phaseout behavior is meant to be complete
- the model should either compute the phaseout or mark the phaseout branch as an explicit limitation
- mortgage-interest amounts from `1098` flow naturally into Schedule A, but Schedule A remains the authority for what is deductible there
- itemizing and standard deduction are competing branches; the software should compare them, not assume itemizing whenever values exist
- some expenses belong elsewhere and should not be double-counted on Schedule A

Publication cross-references worth preserving:

- `Pub. 502` for medical and dental detail
- `Pub. 504` for MFS treatment of joint expenses
- `Pub. 936` for mortgage-interest detail
- `Pub. 969` for HSA / MSA-related medical interaction

Audit cues for current model:

- review `f1040sa.5e` against the 2025 high-income phaseout language
- review any mortgage-interest handling to make sure refunds of overpaid interest and points are treated consistently with the instruction flow
