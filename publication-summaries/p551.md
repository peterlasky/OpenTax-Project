# P551

Source file: `forms-instructions-and-publications/publications/p551.pdf`

Title: `Publication 551 - Basis of Assets`

Role in the tax program:

- foundational basis reference used across depreciation, casualty loss, gain/loss, gifts, inheritance, and property conversions
- one of the most important publications for getting long-term tax calculations right

Core principle:

- basis is taxpayer’s investment in property for tax purposes
- basis is used for:
  - depreciation
  - amortization
  - depletion
  - casualty losses
  - gain/loss on disposition

Main sections:

- cost basis
- adjusted basis
- basis other than cost

Core rule:

- purchased property basis is usually cost
- cost can include certain acquisition-related amounts that must be capitalized

Examples of cost-basis components:

- sales tax
- freight
- installation/testing
- excise taxes
- legal/accounting fees when capitalized
- recording fees
- assumed real estate taxes in some cases

Adjusted-basis rule:

- basis increases for improvements and certain additional capital costs
- basis decreases for depreciation, casualty losses, and other recoveries/adjustments

Special basis categories:

- stocks and bonds
- real property
- business assets
- allocated asset purchases
- property received for services
- taxable exchanges
- nontaxable exchanges
- spouse transfers
- gifts
- inherited property
- personal property converted to business/rental use

2025 items called out:

- small businesses with average annual gross receipts of `31 million` or less over prior 3 years are generally exempt from UNICAP if not tax shelters
- inherited-property basis must be consistent with estate-tax valuation where section `1014(f)` applies

Critical modeling implications:

- basis cannot be a static field
- it must be event-driven over time
- many later calculations fail if basis history is not preserved

Model-building takeaways:

- every asset/property record should support:
  - original acquisition method
  - original basis source
  - later capital additions
  - depreciation / amortization reductions
  - casualty / reimbursement adjustments
  - gift / inherited / spouse-transfer carryover or stepped-up basis logic
  - conversion-to-business/rental basis rules
- treat basis as first-class reference data, not a derived afterthought
