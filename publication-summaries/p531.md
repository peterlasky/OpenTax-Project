# P531

Source file: `forms-instructions-and-publications/publications/p531.pdf`

Title: `Publication 531 - Reporting Tip Income`

Role in the tax program:

- focused guide for employee tip reporting
- relevant to wage intake, unreported-tip corrections, employer reporting, and allocated-tip handling

Core rule:

- all tips are income and subject to federal income tax

Types of tips included:

- direct cash tips
- charged tips paid by employer
- tip-pool / tip-splitting amounts retained
- noncash tips

Required workflow:

- keep daily tip record
- report tips to employer
- report all tips on tax return

Important distinctions:

- service charge is not a tip if customer lacks unrestricted choice over the payment
- service charges paid to worker are wages, not tips
- noncash tips are income, but are not reported to employer the same way as cash/check/card tips

Employer-reporting threshold:

- if total tips from one job are less than `20` for a month, they are not reported to employer for that month

Why employer reporting matters:

- withholding of income tax and payroll taxes
- proper SSA / Railroad Retirement earnings credit
- avoidance of penalty for not reporting tips to employer

Core model-building rules:

- distinguish:
  - tips reported to employer
  - tips not reported to employer
  - noncash tips
  - service charges treated as wages
  - allocated tips
- do not collapse service charges into tip income
- do not require employer reporting of noncash tips, but still include them on return where required

Historical/process notes:

- publication is now continuous-use rather than annual
- old `Form 4070` / `4070A` are historical, but their information requirements still matter operationally

Model-building takeaways:

- capture tip records at a more granular level than a single annual total if possible
- preserve enough detail to support:
  - payroll-tax reconciliation
  - uncollected tax situations
  - allocated-tip prompts
  - correct wage-vs-tip classification
