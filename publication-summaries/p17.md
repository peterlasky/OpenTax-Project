# P17

Source file: `forms-instructions-and-publications/publications/p17.pdf`

Title: `Publication 17 - Your Federal Income Tax For Individuals`

Role in the tax program:

- broad individual-return master guide
- useful for core filing rules, thresholds, standard deduction, filing status, dependents, withholding, income categories, and high-level credit routing
- good “default rulebook” when a narrower publication is not more specific

2025 items called out near the front:

- return due date: `April 15, 2026`
- standard deduction amounts:
  - single / MFS: `15,750`
  - MFJ / QSS: `31,500`
  - HOH: `23,625`
- new `Trump account` / `Form 4547` concept for certain children born after 2024 and before 2029
- main-home-in-the-United-States checkbox matters for benefits like EIC
- dependents section changed and now asks for more detail to support IRS benefit determinations
- write-in items now often have dedicated boxes / entry areas instead of freeform annotations
- if taxpayer died before filing, use deceased box and date-of-death handling on the return
- governmental paid family leave contributions are included in income; if itemizing, may be part of SALT
- `Form 1099-DA` appears for digital asset broker reporting

Core structure of the publication:

- filing information
- filing status
- dependents
- withholding / estimated tax
- wages / earnings
- interest income
- social security benefits
- other income
- IRAs
- standard deduction
- taxes
- itemized deductions
- how to figure tax
- child tax credit / other dependent credit
- tax table / tax computation worksheet / tax rate schedules

Highest-value build rules:

- start with filing obligation, filing status, and dependent determination before computing downstream benefits
- standard deduction, credits, and some eligibility tests are filing-status dependent
- many line-level return decisions are driven by facts that are collected above line 1 on the form
- use the `2025 Tax Table`, `2025 Tax Computation Worksheet`, and `2025 Tax Rate Schedules` as the line `16` backbone when no narrower special worksheet overrides

Filing information implications:

- who must file depends on income level, status, age, and special taxes
- filing deadline, extension logic, and deceased-filer handling should be modeled at least as factual inputs / workflow notes
- withholding and estimated-tax logic should be separate from ordinary income computation

Filing status implications:

- core statuses:
  - single
  - married filing jointly
  - married filing separately
  - head of household
  - qualifying surviving spouse
- status affects:
  - filing threshold
  - standard deduction
  - tax table / brackets
  - credit eligibility
- status selection should remain a one-hot source worksheet choice, not a freeform field

Dependents implications:

- dependent determination drives:
  - child tax credit
  - credit for other dependents
  - earned income credit
  - filing / support questions
- the expanded dependents section on the 2025 form means the data model should keep enough detail to support IRS eligibility checks, not just name/SSN

Income implications:

- wages, interest, social security, and “other income” each have separate source and worksheet paths
- do not flatten all income into one generic number before preserving source type
- social security benefits need separate total-benefits vs taxable-benefits handling
- when `Pub. 17` points to special social-security worksheets, the source path is explicit:
  - `Worksheet 1. Figuring Your Taxable Benefits` in `Pub. 915`
  - `Worksheet 2. Figure Your Additional Taxable Benefits (From a Lump-Sum Payment for a Year After 1993)` in `Pub. 915`
  - `Worksheet 3. Figure Your Additional Taxable Benefits (From a Lump-Sum Payment for a Year Before 1994)` in `Pub. 915`
  - `Worksheet 4. Figure Your Taxable Benefits Under the Lump-Sum Election Method (Use With Worksheet 2 or 3)` in `Pub. 915`
- IRA rules interact with contributions, distributions, deductions, and age
- when IRA deduction and Social Security interact, `Pub. 17` points to `Appendix B` of `Pub. 590-A`:
  - `Worksheet 1. Computation of Modified AGI (for use only by taxpayers who receive social security benefits)`
  - `Worksheet 2` to figure how much traditional IRA contribution can be deducted
  - `Worksheet 3` to figure how much Social Security is taxable after the IRA interaction

Deduction implications:

- standard deduction is a major branch and must use current-year amounts
- itemized deduction logic remains a separate branch, not just a single overrideable amount
- governmental paid family leave contributions now have special income/itemized treatment

Tax / credit implications:

- line `16` ordinary tax should come from:
  - `2025 Tax Table`
  - `2025 Tax Computation Worksheet`
  - narrower special worksheets where another source explicitly requires them
- child tax credit and credit for other dependents are important enough to keep as explicit modeled paths
- EIC gating should consider the “main home in U.S.” and dependent facts

Model-building takeaways:

- Pub. 17 is a high-level coordination guide; use it to validate the full return flow
- it is especially useful for:
  - thresholds
  - filing status logic
  - standard deduction values
  - line-routing sanity checks
- when another publication gives narrower rules, that narrower publication should win
