# P504

Source file: `forms-instructions-and-publications/publications/p504.pdf`

Title: `Publication 504 - Divorced or Separated Individuals`

Role in the tax program:

- main guide for divorce/separation effects on:
  - filing status
  - dependency-related benefits
  - alimony / child support
  - property transfers
  - IRA transfers
  - withholding / estimated tax
  - community-property complications

Most important modeling use:

- this publication explains when marital breakup facts change return routing, not just one deduction line

Core topics highlighted in the introduction:

- choosing filing status
- deciding which benefits can be claimed
- alimony
- child support
- other court-ordered payments
- property settlements
- IRA transfers
- deduction treatment for some divorce costs
- withholding / estimated tax handling
- community property state rules

Filing-status backbone:

- filing status depends largely on marital status on the last day of the tax year
- if unmarried at year-end, possible statuses include:
  - single
  - head of household
  - qualifying surviving spouse
- if married at year-end, status is generally:
  - married filing jointly
  - married filing separately
- there is an exception path where living apart may allow treatment as unmarried for HOH purposes

Marital-status rules worth encoding:

- final decree of divorce or separate maintenance by year-end => unmarried for the whole year
- annulment means no valid marriage existed; prior affected years may require amended returns
- interlocutory decree is not enough by itself
- registered domestic partnership / civil union that is not “marriage” under law is not marriage for federal tax purposes

Important exception:

- if spouses divorce purely to file as unmarried and intend to remarry and do remarry the next tax year, they must still file as married for the divorce year

Dependency / child-related implications:

- dependent SSN/TIN required
- `Form 8332` matters for release / revocation of child-related claim rights
- divorce can separate:
  - custodial-parent facts
  - who gets dependency-linked benefits
  - who gets child-related tax benefits
- do not assume all child benefits move together automatically just because one parent claims something

Relief / spouse-liability implications:

- joint-return liability relief is a major branch
- keep support for:
  - innocent spouse / similar relief
  - injured spouse allocation
- relevant forms:
  - `8857`
  - `8379`
- `Pub. 971` is an important companion

PTC / marketplace implications:

- marital-status changes should be reported to Marketplace during the year
- if divorced or legally separated during the year and previously on same qualified health plan, policy amounts may need allocation across separate returns
- `Pub. 974` cross-reference matters here

Withholding / estimated-tax implications:

- taxpayer must furnish updated `Form W-4` within `10` days after divorce or separation if prior withholding assumed spouse allowance
- estimated-tax and payment allocations are part of the divorce/separation logic and should not be treated as an afterthought

Property / transfer implications:

- property settlements and transfers between spouses / former spouses must be tracked distinctly from ordinary sales
- IRA transfers incident to divorce have special treatment; do not model them as ordinary taxable distributions by default
- `Pub. 544`, `590-A`, and `590-B` are important supporting authorities

Community-property implications:

- living in community-property states can override naive “who earned / who paid” assumptions
- keep a separate community-property fact branch rather than blending it into ordinary MFJ/MFS logic
- `Pub. 555` is the companion publication

Administrative identity implications:

- divorce/separation often changes:
  - name
  - address
  - withholding certificate
  - dependent claim rights
- ITIN / SSN / address-change handling is specifically called out

Model-building takeaways:

- divorce/separation is not just a filing-status toggle
- schema should preserve:
  - marital-status-at-year-end
  - dates of divorce/separation/annulment
  - whether spouses lived apart
  - custodial-parent status
  - child-claim release / revocation
  - alimony / child support characterization
  - property transfer type
  - PTC policy allocation facts
  - community-property state applicability
  - relief-from-joint-liability path
