# P527

Source file: `forms-instructions-and-publications/publications/p527.pdf`

Title: `Publication 527 - Residential Rental Property (Including Rental of Vacation Homes)`

Role in the tax program:

- primary guide for residential rental income/expense treatment
- central for `Schedule E` residential rentals, vacation homes, rental depreciation, passive-loss limits, and mixed personal/rental use

Core structure:

- rental income / expenses when no personal use
- depreciation
- reporting rental income and deductions
- special rental situations
- vacation-home / personal-use rules

High-value 2025 items:

- standard mileage rate: `70 cents` per mile
- bonus depreciation restored to `100%` for qualified property acquired and placed in service after `2025-01-19`
- section 179 max: `2,500,000`, phaseout starts above `4,000,000`
- SALT cap increased to `40,000` (`20,000` MFS), phased down above `500,000` MAGI (`250,000` MFS), not below `10,000` (`5,000` MFS)
- NIIT may apply to rental/passive income
- excess business loss limitation may route disallowed loss through `Form 461`

Core modeling rules:

- all rental income is generally reported
- deductibility depends heavily on:
  - profit motive
  - personal use
  - passive-loss limits
  - depreciation rules
- residential rental with no personal use is different from vacation-home mixed-use property

Main distinction to preserve:

- rental-for-profit, no personal use
- mixed personal/rental use
- not-for-profit rental
- property converted to rental use
- part-of-home rental

Main forms/publications connected:

- `Schedule E`
- `Form 4562`
- `Form 8582`
- `Form 461`
- `Form 8960`
- `Pub. 523`
- `Pub. 544`
- `Pub. 547`
- `Pub. 551`
- `Pub. 925`
- `Pub. 946`

Vacation-home implications:

- personal-use days matter
- expense allocation between personal and rental use matters
- income may still be taxable even where expense deduction is limited
- do not model vacation rentals as ordinary full-deduction rentals without a personal-use test

Depreciation implications:

- rental property basis and placed-in-service dates matter
- land vs building must stay separate
- improvements and property classes matter

Loss-limitation implications:

- passive-activity rules can defer rental losses
- excess business loss limitation can further constrain reported loss
- NIIT can still pick up passive rental income

Model-building takeaways:

- preserve for each property:
  - ownership/use dates
  - days rented
  - days personal use
  - basis / land split
  - improvements
  - expenses by category
  - materially participate / passive status indicators
- cross-link home sale and rental history because former main homes and mixed-use homes require coordinated treatment
