# P525

Source file: `forms-instructions-and-publications/publications/p525.pdf`

Title: `Publication 525 - Taxable and Nontaxable Income`

Role in the tax program:

- broad inclusion/exclusion guide for income classification
- important when deciding whether an amount belongs in taxable income at all
- useful for many miscellaneous line-routing decisions not fully covered by narrower publications

Core principle:

- income is taxable unless specifically excluded by law
- nontaxable amounts may still need to be disclosed or tracked

Major topic areas from the publication:

- employee compensation
- special rules for certain employees
- business and investment income
- sickness and injury benefits
- miscellaneous income
- repayments

2025 items called out:

- possible deduction for overtime above regular rate
- possible deduction for qualified tips
- deferred compensation contribution limits increased
- health FSA salary-reduction limit: `3,300`
- qualified wildfire relief payments may be nontaxable
- East Palestine derailment relief payments may be nontaxable
- rollover path from long-term qualified tuition program to Roth IRA exists, subject to restrictions

Very important general tax-engine rules:

- constructive receipt matters:
  - income available to taxpayer can be taxable even before physical possession
- assignment-of-income doctrine matters:
  - directing payment to someone else does not necessarily avoid inclusion
- advance payments are generally income when received, subject to accounting-method rules

Foreign-income rule:

- U.S. citizen / resident alien generally reports worldwide income unless specifically exempt
- foreign wages, interest, dividends, capital gains, pensions, rents, and royalties are all potentially includible
- foreign earned income exclusion path belongs to `Pub. 54`, not this publication alone

Model-building implications:

- do not decide taxability purely from whether a form was received
- preserve classification layer:
  - taxable
  - excluded
  - deferred
  - partially taxable
  - repayment-sensitive
- build support for constructive-receipt timing and assignment-of-income edge cases where material

Employee compensation implications:

- wages and fringe benefits may be taxable or excludable depending on specific rule
- qualified equity grants, overtime, tips, and FSAs all need explicit handling rather than generic wage intake

Sickness/injury implications:

- some benefits can be excluded while others are taxable depending on source and prior tax treatment
- do not flatten disability / sick-pay / insurance proceeds into one rule

Repayment implications:

- if taxpayer repays an amount previously included in income, treatment may require a special calculation path rather than a simple negative income entry

Model-building takeaways:

- use Pub. 525 as the “should this be in gross income?” reference
- when amount is not obviously wages / interest / dividends / capital gain, check this publication before inventing a line
- preserve enough source metadata to support taxability decisions later
